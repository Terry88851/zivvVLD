01 · Strategy

Think like a 1600 scorer.

At the top end, random practice is inefficient. Use a loop: diagnose → learn → drill → review → retest.

1

Take a diagnostic

Start with a full, timed practice test. Record every wrong answer and every question you guessed on.

2

Build an error log

Classify mistakes: concept gap, careless error, timing, misread, vocabulary, or answer-choice trap.

3

Target weaknesses

Spend most of your study time on recurring weaknesses rather than topics you already know.

4

Drill precisely

Do short sets focused on one skill. Accuracy first; speed comes after the method becomes automatic.

5

Review deeply

For every mistake, explain why the correct answer works and why your choice fails.

6

Retest

Return to the same skill later. A weakness isn't fixed until you can solve it consistently under time pressure.

02 · 8 weeks

A simple weekly progression.

Adjust the workload to your schedule. Consistent, focused practice beats exhausting study sessions.

WEEK 1–2

Diagnose

  • Take a full test
  • Analyze every miss
  • List top 5 weaknesses
WEEK 3–4

Repair

  • Review core concepts
  • Skill-based drills
  • Build your error log
WEEK 5–6

Accelerate

  • Timed mixed sets
  • Practice harder questions
  • Reduce careless errors
WEEK 7–8

Polish

  • Full timed tests
  • Analyze score patterns
  • Protect accuracy

What usually separates “high” from “1600”?

When your score is already strong, the biggest gains often come from consistency—not simply learning more content.

Careless errorsSlow down on the final step and verify what the question actually asks.
Unreviewed practiceA completed question set has little value if you never study why you missed questions.
Timing panicUse timed mini-sets before jumping into full tests.
Weak fundamentalsFix the underlying rule or concept instead of memorizing one question.

1600 is a process, not a trick.

Set a baseline, find the patterns in your mistakes, and make each practice session solve a specific problem.
